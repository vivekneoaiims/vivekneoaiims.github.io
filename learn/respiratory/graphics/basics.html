Module 1

Why graphics?

The screen shows what the baby's lungs are doing, breath by breath.

2 am. Kabir's ventilator alarms: "Low expiratory volume." His SpO₂ is drifting down. The nurse glances at the screen and says, "The loop isn't closing. It's a leak."

She diagnosed it in two seconds, without a blood gas. That's the skill.

Why graphics?

  • Gives an objective picture of treatment
  • Helps optimise ventilator settings
  • Picks up misadventures like tube leak and secretions
  • Shows the effect of treatment (surfactant, suction)
  • Helps the weaning process

Classification

● Scalars

Time on the x-axis, against pressure, volume or flow.

● Loops

Pressure against volume (P-V loop). Volume against flow (flow-volume loop).

Our aim: to identify
  1. Leak
  2. Air trapping (auto-PEEP)
  3. Secretions
  4. Beaking (excess pressure)
  5. Compliance

What to look forThe peak is the tidal volume. If the line doesn't come back to zero, gas went in but didn't come out: a leak.

Flow vs time

The most useful scalarExpiratory flow must reach zero before the next breath. If it doesn't, the lung hasn't emptied: air trapping.

What did that change do?

Change one setting at a time and say out loud what moved on the screen.

  • What effect did the change in Ti have? (Too short: flow is cut off before it reaches zero, so volume falls.)
  • What happened to volume when you raised PIP? (Bigger breath, until the lung is over-stretched.)

Module 3

Pressure–volume loop

Pressure on the x-axis, volume on the y-axis. One breath draws one loop.

Components of the P-V loop

The loop starts at PEEP (bottom left) and climbs to PIP and VT (top right).

Inspiration is the lower limb, expiration the upper limb: on a machine breath the loop runs anticlockwise.

The slope of the line joining the two ends is the compliance. Steep = soft lung. Flat = stiff lung.

PEEP and the P-V loop

Over-distension (beaking)

Paw rises with little or no change in VT. The top of the loop flattens into a beak.

The lung is full. Extra pressure only stretches and injures it.

FixLower the PIP until the beak disappears.

Type of breath on the P-V loop

I: inspiration · E: expiration. A baby breathing on its own makes negative pressure, so its loop sits left of the axis.

Module 4

Flow–volume loop

Volume on the x-axis, flow on the y-axis. Inspiration above the line, expiration below.

Components of the flow-volume loop

FRC: the breath starts and ends here (left end).

PIFR: peak inspiratory flow, the top. PEFR: peak expiratory flow, the bottom.

VT: the right end, where inspiration turns into expiration.

Increased airway resistance

The expiratory limb is "scooped out" and PEFR falls: gas can't get out fast through a narrow pipe.

Think: secretions, a kinked or small tube, meconium, BPD.
